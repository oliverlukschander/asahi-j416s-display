## Identifying the downstream hop

`/sys/kernel/debug/thunderbolt/0-1/port1/path` was decoded directly (all
hub ports 1-23 scanned) to find which hop actually carries our
`in_counter_index=0` marker, rather than assuming a port number in advance.
Result: hub port1 (the hub's own upstream link-in adapter, confirmed as
such since its other active hops there also carry the host<->hub AUX/USB3
traffic), hop id10: `out_port=19 next_hop=9 counter=0 counter_enable=1`.
`out_port=19` is the DP OUT adapter, and `counter=0,counter_enable=1`
matches exactly what0107's `tb_dp_init_video_path` sets -- this is
unambiguously the video path's downstream hop.

## Counter readings

```
DP-IN hop (route0-0 port5, counter idx0):        0x0000a465 (42085)
Downstream hop (route0-1 port1 hop10, idx0):     0x0001765b (95835)
```

Both nonzero. `tb_path_activate()` clears each enabled counter to0 at every
path activation, and nothing else in this driver ever turns these on for a
DP tunnel, so both readings are new, direct hardware evidence from this
exact attempt.

## What this establishes

Video-path traffic is not stopping at the host, and it is not stopping in
the USB4 tunnel: it demonstrably reaches the hub-side hop whose entire
purpose is forwarding to the DP OUT adapter (`out_port=19`). Combined with
0104's evidence that the DP OUT adapter's own CS registers already report
4 lanes/HBR negotiated and DPRX done, and with the standing fact that this
exact monitor+cable+VMM7100+hub combination is confirmed working under
macOS, the crossbar/DCP/tunnel path built across0102-0107 is no longer a
credible explanation for the missing picture. The counters do not by
themselves prove the DP OUT adapter forwards this traffic onward to its
physical Type-C jack correctly, or that the hub's own physical DP-alt-mode
link to the VMM7100 ever trains -- that stage is outside anything the
Linux Thunderbolt/Apple driver stack directly controls or can currently
observe; it is internal hub firmware/silicon behavior.

## Not yet checked

DP tunnel bandwidth allocation mode (`tb_dp_bandwidth_alloc_mode_enable`,
generic tunnel.c, already present, currently opt-in based on
`usb4_dp_port_bandwidth_mode_supported()`) was not confirmed active or
inactive for this tunnel in either this or prior captures -- the relevant
`tb_tunnel_dbg` lines are pr_debug-level and were not visible at default
log verbosity. Whether BW allocation mode negotiates and whether the hub
expects/requires a real bandwidth grant before it will drive its physical
output is an open, unexplored question, distinct from anything ruled in or
out in0102-0107. No conclusion is drawn about it yet; it needs read-only
investigation (dynamic debug or a targeted log line) before any new
hardware step, not another hotplug this boot.
